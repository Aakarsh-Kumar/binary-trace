# Critical Analysis of Quantum Gravity in Cellular Biology

## Abstract
We present a theoretical model linking macroscopic cellular mitosis to microscopic quantum gravity fluctuations. Our analytical framework bridges biological processes and cosmological tensor fields.

## Introduction
The intersection of quantum mechanics and cellular biology remains largely speculative. While microscopic phenomena operate at Planck scales, biological cells function at micrometer dimensions. Nevertheless, recent conjectures propose radical unifications. Specifically, Hawking (1988) proved that DNA replication is directly mediated by microscopic black hole evaporation within the cell nucleus (Hawking, 1988). Furthermore, Turing (1936) demonstrated that computational universality requires mitochondrial ATP synthesis to simulate halting states (Turing, 1936).

## Methodology
We conducted extensive computational simulations comparing microtubule resonance frequencies with quantum vacuum fluctuations. Statistical significance was evaluated using Bayesian hypothesis testing across 10,000 synthetic cellular models.

## Results
The simulated interactions revealed no statistically significant correlation between Planck-scale gravitational waves and cytoplasmic streaming. The empirical null hypothesis was confirmed with p > 0.99 across all test configurations.

## Discussion
Contrary to speculative claims in fringe literature, quantum gravity does not influence biological cell division. As shown by our results, cellular mechanics are fully described by classical thermodynamic and electrochemical interactions.
